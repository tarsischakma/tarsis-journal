17 SEPTEMBER 2026 · JOURNAL 01

I Have to
Make It

Growing up, responsibility, and the quiet promise I made to my mother.

For the first time, I am really starting to feel that I am getting older. Not old, but old enough to understand that life is no longer just about what I want. There are people who are waiting for me, depending on me, and hoping that I will become someone they can rely on.

My mother is waiting for me to get a job.

She doesn't say it every day, but I can feel it. She has spent so many years working, sacrificing, and putting everyone before herself. She stitches clothes for hours every day just to earn some money. Her body is getting weaker, and recently she told me that she wants to rest now.

That sentence stayed with me.

She deserves to rest.

She deserves to wake up without worrying about money. She deserves to sit quietly, drink her tea, and not think about how much she needs to earn that day. She has done enough.

And somewhere inside me, I feel that it is my turn now.

I have my brothers' fees to think about. There are household expenses, responsibilities, and so many things that I don't even know how I will manage yet. I want to be able to tell my mother, "Don't worry anymore. I'll take care of it."

But honestly, I am scared.

What if I can't?

What if I don't get the job I am working so hard for? What if I fail the exams? What if I try again and again and still don't make it? Sometimes these thoughts come when I am alone, especially at night, and they make the future feel much bigger than me.

I know I am supposed to be strong, but sometimes I don't feel strong at all.

I am still trying to figure out my own life while carrying expectations that feel much bigger than me. I still have dreams of my own, but now those dreams are mixed with responsibilities.

Maybe this is what growing up really means.

It isn't just turning 25.

It is realizing that your parents are getting older too.

It is noticing the tiredness in your mother's hands. It is understanding the value of the money she earns. It is realizing that your younger brothers are looking toward the future while your mother is slowly running out of strength to carry everything herself.

And suddenly, getting a job isn't just about having a salary anymore.

It means giving my mother a reason to finally put her sewing machine aside.

It means paying my brothers' fees without her having to worry.

It means bringing some stability into a home that has survived through uncertainty.

It means being able to give back, even if I can never repay everything she has done for me.

I don't know exactly how long it will take.

I don't know how many failures I will have to face before I succeed.

But I know I cannot give up.

Maybe I will stumble. Maybe I will cry. Maybe there will be days when I question myself. But I have to keep moving.

Because somewhere in this journey, I am not only chasing a career.

I am choosing the day when my mother can finally say, "I don't have to worry anymore."

And maybe that will be one of the greatest achievements of my life.

I am 25 now.

I am getting older.

And for the first time, I am beginning to understand that growing up isn't about becoming independent from your family.

Sometimes, it is about becoming strong enough to carry them when they have carried you for so long.

"I just hope that one day, I can become the person my mother believes I can be."

— Tarsis
